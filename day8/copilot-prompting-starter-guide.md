# Copilot Prompting Starter Guide
### For FinBridge Office Staff

Welcome to Microsoft 365 Copilot. This guide will help you get useful, accurate results straight away — no technical knowledge needed. The key is knowing how to ask.

## The Simple Formula for a Good Prompt

Think of every prompt in four parts. You don't need all four every time, but the more you include, the better the result:

> **Role + Task + Context + Format**

| Part | What it means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Role** | Tell Copilot who it's acting as | *"You are a professional business writer…"* |
| **Task** | What you want it to do | *"…summarise this email thread…"* |
| **Context** | The background it needs | *"…for a colleague who missed the meeting…"* |
| **Format** | How you want the output | *"…in three bullet points."* |

Put together: **"Summarise this email thread for a colleague who missed the meeting. Use three bullet points and keep it under 100 words."**

You don't need all four parts every time. Even just adding a bit of context — who the audience is, or how long you want the answer — makes a noticeable difference. Start simple and add more detail if the first result isn't quite what you needed.

## Copilot in Outlook

### Example 1 — Summarise a long email thread
> "Summarise this email thread. List the key decisions made, any open actions, and who owns each one."

Use this when you've come back from leave or been copied into a long chain and need to catch up fast.

---

### Example 2 — Draft a reply
> "Draft a polite reply saying I can attend the 3pm Thursday meeting but need it to finish by 4pm. Keep it short and professional."

Copilot will write the reply for you. Review it, tweak anything that doesn't sound like you, then send.

---

### Example 3 — Rewrite a message for a different audience
> "Rewrite this email so it is suitable for a senior executive. Remove the technical detail and focus on the business impact and the decision we need from them."

Paste in your draft, and Copilot will adjust the tone and content for you.

## Copilot in Word

### Example 1 — Draft a first version of a document
> "Write a one-page agenda for a project kick-off meeting. The project is a migration of our finance team to new payroll software. Include sections for introductions, project overview, timelines, risks, and next steps."

You'll have a solid first draft in seconds. Edit it to match your team's style.

---

### Example 2 — Improve existing text
> "Rewrite this paragraph so it is clearer and more concise. The audience is non-technical office managers. Remove any jargon."

Highlight the text you want to improve, then click the Copilot icon in the Word toolbar (it looks like a small sparkle or star). A sidebar will open — type or paste your prompt there and press Enter. Copilot will suggest a rewritten version for you to accept or tweak.

---

### Example 3 — Create a summary or executive overview
> "Write a short executive summary for this document. Keep it to three paragraphs: what the issue is, what we are doing about it, and what outcome we expect. Plain language only."

Great for adding a summary page to a report before sending it to leadership.

---

## Copilot in Excel

### Example 1 — Explain what's in a spreadsheet
> "Look at this data and tell me what it shows. Point out any figures that look unusual or different from the rest."

Copilot will describe what it sees in plain English — it won't change or colour your spreadsheet. Useful when you've been handed a file you didn't build and need to get up to speed quickly.

---

### Example 2 — Create a formula
> "Write a formula that adds up all values in column C where the corresponding value in column A is 'Finance'. Explain what the formula does in plain English."

You don't need to know Excel formulas — just describe what you want in normal language.

---

### Example 3 — Summarise data as a short report
> "Summarise this spreadsheet as a short written report suitable for a weekly update email. Include the total, the highest value, the lowest value, and any notable trends. Three paragraphs maximum."

Turns rows and columns into a readable update you can paste straight into an email.

## A Note on Data Safety

Good news first: Microsoft 365 Copilot is connected to your FinBridge work account and has been approved by IT. Everything you do inside it stays within FinBridge — it works the same way your work email does. You do not need to worry about using it for everyday work tasks.

The one thing to be aware of is **external AI tools** — things like the free public versions of ChatGPT or similar websites you might find on Google. Those tools are not connected to FinBridge, and anything you type into them can potentially be stored or used by the company that runs them. So please treat them the same way you'd treat a public photocopier: fine for general things, but not for anything sensitive.

As a rule of thumb (and this was a key habit from Day 1 of this programme): if you wouldn't email it to a stranger, don't paste it into a public AI tool. That means things like:
- Client names or account details
- Salary or financial records
- Internal reports or contracts

If you're ever not sure whether a tool is approved, just drop IT a quick message before using it — we're happy to help.

## Quick Tips

- **Be specific.** "Write an email" gives a generic result. "Write a short, friendly email to a client confirming their invoice has been received and will be processed within five working days" gives you something usable.
- **Keep going.** If the first result isn't quite right, just follow up in the same chat: *"Make it shorter"* or *"Make the tone more formal"* or *"Add a bullet point about the deadline."* You can go back and forth as many times as you like — it's a conversation, not a one-shot test.
- **You are always in control.** Copilot gives you a draft. You review it, edit it, and send it. Never skip the review step.
- **Always give it a quick read.** Copilot is very good at structure and tone, but it can occasionally get a fact, figure, or date wrong. It's the same as using a spell-checker — helpful, but you're still the one responsible for what goes out. A 30-second read-through before you send is all it takes.
